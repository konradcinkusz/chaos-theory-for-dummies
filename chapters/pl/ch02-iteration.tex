\chapter{Reguła następnego kroku}
\label{ch:iteration}

\chapterstub{%
\textit{[Opis do przetłumaczenia --- patrz wydanie angielskie.]}

Argument: the most important idea in the book is iteration \dash{} a rule
that turns today's state into tomorrow's, applied again and again:
\code{x[n+1] = f(x[n])}. Savings at compound interest, a drug washing out of
the blood, a cup cooling towards room temperature and a population with a
fixed birth rate are all the same shape, \code{x[n+1] = a*x[n] + b}, and
that shape has only three behaviours: grow without bound, shrink to nothing,
or settle at a fixed point. Exponential growth and decay, doubling time and
half-life, and the logarithm as the question how many steps until. Fixed
points are where the rule leaves the state unchanged, and a fixed point is
STABLE when a small nudge shrinks, which for this linear rule means the
multiplier has size below one. The cobweb diagram is introduced here on a
straight line, so that Chapter 5 can use it on a curve. Payoff: the reader
can predict, without iterating, what a linear rule will do forever \dash{}
which is exactly the certainty the rest of the book takes away. Traps to
elicit: exponential growth means fast growth (it means proportional growth,
and it can be slow); a fixed point is where the system ends up (only if it
is stable). Listings: iterating a rule; the doubling-time table; the cobweb
data. One or two labs: find a fixed point and decide its stability; compute
a doubling time with a logarithm. Figures: three linear rules and their
three fates; a cobweb on a line.
