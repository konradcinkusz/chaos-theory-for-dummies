\chapter{Wahadła i planety}
\label{ch:mechanics}

\chapterstub{%
\textit{[Opis do przetłumaczenia --- patrz wydanie angielskie.]}

Argument: the clockwork of Chapter 1 was built on mechanics, and mechanics
is where chaos was first seen. Hang a second pendulum from the first and the
motion becomes chaotic: two double pendulums released a fraction of a degree
apart swing together, then apart, measured here with energy conservation as
the check that the divergence is physics and not numerical error. Poincaré
found the same in the three-body problem while competing for King Oscar II's
prize (the memoir published in 1890), and his corrected work is the first
description of chaos. The solar system itself is chaotic on long timescales:
Laskar's 1989 computation gives a Lyapunov time of about five million years
for the inner planets, which means the positions of the planets cannot be
predicted a hundred million years ahead even though their orbits will stay
roughly where they are for billions \dash{} the attractor idea again, as
bounded unpredictability. Friction-free mechanics conserves energy and so
has no attractor; chaos there is a sea around islands of regular motion,
stated in words (KAM) and shown with a double pendulum at low and high
energy. Payoff: the reader can simulate a double pendulum, check energy
conservation, measure divergence, and separate the parts of the solar-system
story that are measured from those that are folklore. Traps to elicit: the
solar system is a perfect clock; a system that conserves energy cannot be
chaotic; chaos needs friction or randomness. Listings: the double pendulum
with RK4 and an energy check; divergence of two releases; low against high
energy. Labs: implement the energy function; find the time two releases
separate. Figures: two double pendulum tips diverging; energy error against
time.
