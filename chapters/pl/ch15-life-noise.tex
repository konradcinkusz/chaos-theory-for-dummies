\chapter{Życie, rynki i szum: jak odróżnić chaos od przypadku}
\label{ch:life}

\chapterstub{%
\textit{[Opis do przetłumaczenia --- patrz wydanie angielskie.]}

Argument: chaos is claimed for populations, epidemics, hearts, brains and
markets, and the reader should be able to judge the claims. Populations:
May's map was written for ecology, and Costantino and colleagues' 1997
flour-beetle experiment in Science pushed a real population through period
doubling into chaos by manipulating adult mortality. Epidemics: the basic
SIR model settles, but seasonal forcing (children returning to school) can
make it chaotic, which is one reading of pre-vaccine measles records. Hearts
and brains: rhythms, arrhythmias, and why healthy variability is not the
same as chaos (stated as contested). Markets: prices are irregular, and
irregular is not evidence of chaos. The core skill is TELLING CHAOS FROM
NOISE: a chaotic series and a random series can have the same histogram, yet
plotting each value against the next (a return map) shows a curve for the
chaotic series and a cloud for the random one, and a chaotic series is
predictable a step or two ahead by looking up its nearest past neighbours
while noise is not. The reader applies both tests and then applies them to
noisy chaos, where they get harder \dash{} short noisy data can make
anything look like anything. Payoff: the reader can generate chaotic and
random series with the same distribution, tell them apart with a return map
and a nearest-neighbour forecast, and say what evidence a claim of chaos in
real data needs. Traps to elicit: irregular data proves chaos; chaos in
markets means you can predict them; a random-looking sequence must come from
randomness. Listings: logistic series against shuffled series; return maps;
nearest-neighbour forecast error against horizon; a seasonally forced SIR
model. Labs: build a return map test; build a nearest-neighbour predictor.
Figures: two histograms that match and two return maps that do not; forecast
error against horizon for chaos and noise.
